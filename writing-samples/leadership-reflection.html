Individual Reflection: Ultimate Leader Profile Group Project

Reflective writing · Foundations of Leadership coursework

Challenge

One challenge our group faced early on was a breakdown in communication timing during topic selection. I reached out early in the week to weigh in on which section I wanted to work on, but I did not hear back for several days. Once responses arrived, they came in a quick, back-to-back burst, with an expectation of an immediate reply. Because I was not able to respond right away, the topic I had hoped to work on had already been assigned to someone else, and I ended up with a section I had not chosen. This early delay set an uneven pace for the group and meant decisions were sometimes made without everyone's input fully considered.

Key learning

My key takeaway is that groups need to establish communication norms and response-time expectations at the very start of a project, rather than figuring them out reactively once a delay has already caused a problem. In this case, there was no shared understanding of how quickly people were expected to respond or how decisions would proceed if someone had not yet weighed in. That gap in expectations, more than any individual's actions, is what allowed a few days of silence to turn into a missed opportunity for input. It reinforced that a lot of friction in group work is not about intentions, it is about the absence of agreed-upon ground rules.

Actionable idea

Going forward, my actionable idea is to propose a simple team communication agreement at the start of any group project, such as an agreed response window of 24 to 48 hours, along with a plan for how the group will proceed if someone has not responded within that window. Setting this expectation upfront, before any decision is time-sensitive, helps ensure everyone has a fair chance to contribute to choices like topic selection, rather than leaving it to whoever happens to be available first.
